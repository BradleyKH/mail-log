# Scout G-1 display model

A desk model of the Scout G-1 launch vehicle as it flew in 1979 (Algol IIIA, Castor IIA, Antares IIIA, Altair IIIA under a heatshield; MAGSAT rode the first G-1 on 1979-10-30). It stands on a base engraved for Danny K. Harris and his MAIL LOG report for the Scout Project Office at NASA Langley.

Scale is 0.24 mm per inch of vehicle station (about 1:106). Stage lengths, diameters, and the station breaks come from Figure I-1, "Scout Inboard Profile", of the SCOUT User's Manual. Fin planform and heatshield contour are read from that drawing and simplified.

Everything is generated by `v1/build.py` (manifold3d, trimesh, shapely, matplotlib text paths); `v1/render.py` makes the PNG previews.

Assembled height on the base is about 232 mm (rocket about 214 mm).

## Assembly

1. Slide each fin into a slot from the aft end of `rocket_lower` until it stops. The fins hook the same way around the body (pinwheel); they are a sliding fit (0.2 mm per side) and can take a dab of glue.
2. Push the pin of `rocket_lower` into the hole of `rocket_upper`. Sliding fit; glue is optional.
3. Set the aft bore over the post on the base. The fins clear the plate.

## Print settings

- Material: PLA. Layer height 0.16 to 0.2 mm, 0.4 mm nozzle.
- Rocket pieces: print upright, aft end down for `rocket_lower` and the open end down for `rocket_upper`. Use a brim, 3 walls, 15% infill, and slow the outer walls for the slender nose. No supports.
- Fins: print flat on the bed with the flat face down (as exported), 4 copies, 3 walls. No supports.
- Base: print flat, text up. Text is 5 mm to 9 mm tall with strokes of about 1 mm; four 0.2 mm layers of text. Optional filament change at the text layers for contrast. No supports.
- Fit: slots, pin, and post use 0.2 mm clearance per side. If a part binds on your printer, lightly sand or ream rather than re-slicing.

## Hardware

None. Optional cyanoacrylate or PLA-safe glue.
